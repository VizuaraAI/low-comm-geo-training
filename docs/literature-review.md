# Literature review, topic 09: Low-Communication Training across Mixed GPU Clouds

**When one model is trained with DiLoCo-style low communication across mixed GPUs at different cloud providers, which rule for combining the workers' progress works best, does the answer change with the inner optimizer, and what does it cost to reach a fixed loss?**

Prepared on 4 November 2026 by the Vizuara research team. **It was written before Exp01 and Exp02 ran.** The claim stated below is the one those runs were designed to test; see `docs/exp01-results.md` and `docs/exp02-results.md` for what came back, and the README for where the question stands now. The target is a workshop paper, drafted between November 2026 and 1 January 2027. The brief's workshop rows are an ICLR 2027 workshop (first choice; the list appears on 29 November and the suggested deadline is about 1 February), EuroMLSys 2027 (late February, expected), NAIC at SIGCOMM 2027 (May, expected) and CODEC-FM (late August, if it returns).

**The one claim the paper can defend.** On a mixed fleet with measured speeds, the gain from a cleverer aggregation rule shrinks as the inner optimizer whitens its updates. We would show this with a factorial comparison of aggregation rules across inner optimizers, report wall-clock time and dollars to a fixed validation loss for every cell, and test one mechanism: inverse-noise weighting should help AdamW more than Muon, SOAP or SPlus. If there is no such interaction, the result is still usable, because a fleet operator could then choose the rule and the optimizer separately. Neither outcome has been measured yet.

## 1. Positioning

DiLoCo splits training into an inner loop, where each worker takes H local steps, and an outer loop, where workers exchange the change in their weights (the pseudo-gradient) and an outer optimizer applies it. Three lines of work have grown around that design, and they rarely meet.

The first asks how far the method scales on identical workers. Scaling Laws, MuLoCo and How Inner Optimizers Shape Replica Scaling show that the inner optimizer decides how much quality is lost as replicas increase. They use uniform averaging and i.i.d. shards throughout. The second asks how to handle slow or mixed workers: Asynchronous Local-SGD, HALoS, HeLoCo, GeoMesh and Decoupled DiLoCo. All of them run AdamW or Lion as the main inner optimizer, and each tests its own rule against baselines. The third is demonstration runs: INTELLECT-1, Covenant-72B and, on 2 November, Core Automation's 32B model across two clusters. These fix one recipe and report that it works.

Our question sits where the first two lines cross. [1] contains the hook: whitening each worker's updates toward a common covariance makes uniform averaging close to optimal, so a whitening optimizer should need a clever aggregation rule less than AdamW does. [1] also contains a warning that the two axes interact. At H = 1, with plain averaging and no outer momentum, 64 replicas and a 178M model, Muon reaches 3.417 against AdamW's 3.157, the reverse of its H = 30 ordering. The authors hypothesise that this comes from the outer optimizer and do not test it. That is one sentence of evidence that the ranking of inner optimizers depends on the outer setup. Our extension to unequal step counts is our own reasoning and is unproved: unequal H changes the scale of each worker's displacement as well as its shape, so token weights may suffice when shapes already agree.


## 2. Related work

Tags: *closest* (overlaps our question), *baseline* (we run or compare against it), *complementary* (a piece we use or a constraint on our design), *tool*.

### 2.1 DiLoCo and how it scales

- **DiLoCo: Distributed Low-Communication Training of Language Models.** Arthur Douillard et al., 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08105. *Baseline.* AdamW inner, Nesterov outer, C4. With 8 workers it performs as well as fully synchronous training while communicating 500 times less. Robust to data distribution and to workers leaving or joining.
- **Communication-Efficient Language Model Training Scales Reliably and Robustly: Scaling Laws for DiLoCo.** Zachary Charles et al., 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09799. *Complementary.* Under a fixed compute budget, DiLoCo scales predictably with model size and, when tuned, better than data-parallel training. Replica count and token budget are the factors. Identical workers.
- **Understanding Outer Optimizers in Local SGD: Learning Rates, Momentum, and Acceleration.** Ahmed Khaled et al., 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10439. *Complementary.* Convergence theory for the outer step, including what tuning the outer learning rate trades off. The reference for why the outer learning rate must be tuned per cell.
- **What happens when nanochat meets DiLoCo?** Alexander Acker et al., 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13761. *Complementary (warning).* DiLoCo gives competitive pretraining loss but worse MMLU, GSM8K and HumanEval scores after later training stages. Validation loss alone may not be enough, so the main study should add one downstream check.

### 2.2 Inner optimizers inside DiLoCo

- **How Inner Optimizers Shape Replica Scaling.** Eugene Belilovsky, Stefan Horoi, Edouard Oyallon and Benjamin Thérien, 2026 (13 October). https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19064. *Closest.* The brief's [1]. LLaMA-style 178M and 512M models on DCLM, H = 30, Nesterov outer, uniform averaging. Loss increase against each optimizer's own data-parallel run at 178M and 64 replicas: SPlus +3.1%, SOAP +4.8%, Muon +8.5%, AdamW +11%. Measured update-noise anisotropy at 64 replicas (lower is more isotropic), after one local step: AdamW 35, Muon 3.8, SOAP 2.3, SPlus 0.43; over 30 local steps: AdamW 11, Muon 3.3, SOAP 2.5, SPlus 2.4. At H = 30, Muon sits close to the whitening optimizers and far from AdamW, which is why AdamW against Muon is a fair first contrast. No unequal speeds and no alternative weights.
- **MuLoCo: Muon is a practical inner optimizer for DiLoCo.** Benjamin Thérien, Xiaolong Huang, Aaron Defazio, Irina Rish and Eugene Belilovsky, 2025 (v3, June 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23725. *Baseline.* Muon on hidden matrices and AdamW on embeddings, normalization and output layers; 150M to 15B on Nemotron-CC, up to 16 workers. Muon gives more directionally correct pseudo-gradients as workers increase, and it is compatible with quantization, streaming and long intervals. Code: github.com/facebookresearch/MuLoCo. No heterogeneity in the part we read.
- **SOAP, Muon, and Beyond: Pushing LLM Pretraining Scales.** Mikail Khona et al., 2026 (13 July). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20548. *Complementary.* Stabilizes SOAP at large batch sizes and matches update RMS for fair learning-rate transfer. SOAP and Muon beat AdamW on multi-billion-parameter models. Not low-communication, but the update-RMS method is useful for setting learning rates across our arms.
- **Whitening and preconditioned optimizers themselves.** **Scalable Second Order Optimization for Deep Learning** (Distributed Shampoo), Rohan Anil et al., 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09018, is what Core Automation's optimizer derives from. **SOAP: Improving and Stabilizing Shampoo using Adam**, Nikhil Vyas et al., 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11321. **A Stable Whitening Optimizer for Efficient Neural Network Training** (SPlus), Kevin Frans et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07254. **Dobb: Shard-Local Preconditioning in Block-Diagonal Eigenbases**, Shi-Yuk Fong et al., 2026 (29 October), https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39412: GPT-2 XL validation loss 2.476 against Muon's 2.460 and AdamW's 2.497, with a 9.2 ms optimizer call against Muon's 82.7 ms. *Candidates for the third arm.*
- **Stochastic optimization methods through statistical whitening.** Tetiana Parshakova et al., 2026 (17 October). https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24839. *Complementary.* Derives Adam-like, Shampoo-like and Muon-like updates from one statistical view. Useful for explaining our mechanism.
- **Muon as a method.** Keller Jordan's writeup (kellerjordan.github.io/posts/muon/) and the modded-nanogpt speedrun repository (MIT). PyTorch's own source tree now has `torch/optim/_muon.py`. *Tool.* The repository is a useful reference for Muon on small GPTs.

### 2.3 Outer rules and aggregation

- **SNOO: Step-K Nesterov Outer Optimizer.** Dominik Kallusky et al., 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15830. *Complementary.* Single-worker runs show that DiLoCo's benefit comes largely from Nesterov momentum on the pseudo-gradient: compute factor gains of 1.5 to 2.5x up to 1e23 FLOPs. This matters because our "outer rule" arm can be confounded with momentum. Lookahead: k steps forward, 1 step back (Michael R. Zhang et al., 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08610) is the older form.
- **Smoothing DiLoCo with Primal Averaging.** Aaron Defazio et al., 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17131. *Complementary.* Generalized Primal Averaging removes the two-loop structure in the non-distributed setting, with 8.71% to 10.13% faster steps to target over AdamW. Not applicable across islands.
- **Can Model Merging Improve Aggregation in DiLoCo?** Stefan Horoi et al., 2026 (3 July). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03011. *Closest on rules.* IsoLoCo applies Iso-C merging to pseudo-gradients and beats DiLoCo by more as workers increase. Identical workers. In [1], IsoLoCo with AdamW can beat MuLoCo after a beta-2 control, which is itself a hint of interaction.
- **Understanding Stability of Outer Nesterov Momentum on Stochastic Pseudo-Gradients.** Nishant Sharma et al., 2026 (15 October). https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22623. *Complementary (warning).* On random quadratics, the inner batch size decides whether more local steps stabilize or destabilize the outer iteration. Speed-proportional steps and batches may therefore move a worker across a stability boundary.
- **High dimensional theory of two-phase optimizers** (Atish Agarwala, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26954) and **Outer-Momentum Restarting** (Kristi Topollai et al., 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28585). *Complementary.* Theory of noise and momentum in the outer step; restarts widen the stable outer range.
- **AutoLoCo: Communication Efficient Distributed LLM Training via Adaptive Synchronization.** Pengyu He et al., 2026 (29 September). https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36662. *Complementary.* Adapts H over training and corrects the outer step, for 27% lower communication frequency than DiLoCo at matched performance. The same mismatch of steps and outer rule that we study, across time rather than across workers.
- **Federated roots.** **Tackling the Objective Inconsistency Problem in Heterogeneous Federated Optimization** (FedNova), Jianyu Wang et al., 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07481: unequal local steps bias plain averaging. **Adaptive Federated Optimization**, Sashank Reddi et al., 2020, ICLR 2021, https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295. **SlowMo**, Jianyu Wang et al., 2019, ICLR 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.00643. *Tool (definitions).*

### 2.4 Mixed workers, stragglers and asynchrony

- **GeoMesh: Workload-Balanced and Sign-Compressed Geo-Distributed LLM Training.** Changyong Shin et al., 2026 (16 September). Findings of EMNLP 2026 (per the arXiv comment). https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18388. *Closest overall.* Four mixed GPUs (H100, A100, L40S, RTX 6000 Ada) in one region, with a WAN emulated at 580 to 940 Mbps from Azure measurements. Inner steps are proportional to profiled speed; workers send 1-bit signs with token-weighted voting; Lion inner and Nesterov outer. At 150M it is 70.2% faster to target than DiLoCo and 43.8% faster than HALoS, with final perplexity 23.4 against DiLoCo's 23.0. Limits by its own account: bandwidth emulated without latency or jitter, speeds profiled once, one run per configuration.
- **Decoupled DiLoCo for Resilient Distributed Pre-training.** Arthur Douillard et al., 2026 (23 April). https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21428. *Closest on weights.* Learners send fragments to a synchronizer with a minimum quorum, a grace window and a merge weight of tokens times tokens per step; radial-directional averaging is the default merge. Mixed TPU experiment (2B, 8 learners, 18% native speed gap plus 10% variance): quorum 8 gives 84% goodput and text 54.4; quorum 1 with grace gives 100% and text 55.3. In the part we read, AdamW is named only as an example and no weighting ablation is reported.
- **Asynchronous Local-SGD Training for Language Modeling.** Bo Liu et al., 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09135. *Baseline.* The brief's [4]. Up to 150M on C4, simulated speeds, k = 4 to 16, H = 50. Local steps proportional to speed (DyLU) and delayed Nesterov match synchronous perplexity per update. At 150M: DiLoCo 17.23, naive asynchronous 18.08, delayed Nesterov with DyLU 17.26. It compares outer optimizers under AdamW; SGD inner results are not quantified.
- **HeLoCo: Efficient asynchronous low-communication training under data and device heterogeneity.** Abdullah Al Asif et al., 2026 (29 May). https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00271. *Closer.* Corrects stale pseudo-gradients against outer momentum. Five workers, about 15M parameters, multilingual mC4, step times up to 15 times apart. Up to 7.5% better than asynchronous baselines at a fixed token budget and up to 22.1% better than synchronous DiLoCo under severe heterogeneity. Note that its abstract and table disagree on the budget behind the 3.3% figure. Inner optimizer not varied.
- **HALoS: Hierarchical Asynchronous Local SGD over Slow Networks for Geo-Distributed Large Language Model Training.** Geon-Woo Kim et al., 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04531. *Baseline.* Trace-driven simulation of four regions with four workers each, AdamW. Up to 7.5x faster than synchronous baselines and 2.1x faster than other asynchronous methods. Its simulator design is a model for ours.
- **Cross-Facility LLM Pre-training on HPC.** Zarè Palanciyan et al., 2026 (2 October). https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03457. *Closer.* The brief's [2]. Token-weighted Nesterov across H100 and MI250X sites, with global batches of 32 and 64 to fill the barrier. See correction 4. It compares no rules and reports no cost.
- **Cosine-Gated Adam-Decay** (Vatsal Shah and Jiahao Sun, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09126): age-aware outer step, with published Nesterov the least stable under sweep. **Pseudo-Asynchronous Local SGD** (Hiroki Naganuma et al., 2025, TMLR, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18454). **Ringmaster LMO** (Abdurakhmon Sadiev et al., 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18174): asynchronous Muon-style momentum, with an advantage that grows with heterogeneity on NanoChat. **Rennala NSGD** (Igor Sokolov et al., 2026, NeurIPS 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38773): theory beyond Euclidean geometry. **AdLoCo** (Nikolay Kutuzov et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18182): adaptive batches. **Not Every Sync Is Safe** (Maxwell Twelftree et al., 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02544): matched random deferral as a control, a habit worth borrowing. *Complementary.*
- **Topology instead of all-reduce.** **Factored Gossip DiLoCo** (Chamin Hewa Koneputugodage et al., 2026, ICML 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22768), **GASLoC** (Pietro Cagnasso et al., 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11081), **NoLoCo** (Jari Kolehmainen et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10911). *Complementary.* Out of scope for the first paper; GASLoC beats DiLoCo under heterogeneous bandwidth.

### 2.5 Streaming and compression of pseudo-gradients

- **Streaming DiLoCo with overlapping communication.** Arthur Douillard et al., 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18512. *Baseline (later).* Fragment-wise sync, overlap and quantization reduce bandwidth by two orders of magnitude at similar quality. **Eager Updates** (Satyen Kale et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12996) fully overlaps the outer step.
- **Overcoming the Communication-Performance Tradeoff in LLM Pretraining** (SparseLoCo). Amir Sarfi et al., 2025 (v3, July 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15706. *Complementary.* Top-k, 2-bit quantization and error feedback in place of global outer momentum. At 512M, 8 workers, H = 15: DiLoCo 2.76, SparseLoCo at 3.12% density 2.70, payload 17.21 MB against 512.40 MB. AdamW inner, homogeneous data. Removing global momentum also changes the outer rule, so it belongs in a later arm.
- **DeMo: Decoupled Momentum Optimization.** Bowen Peng et al., 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19870. *Complementary.* DCT and top-k on momentum, up to 85x less data per GPU than AdamW DDP. It is the basis Nous cites for **Psyche**, whose post (nousresearch.com/the-next-phase-of-psyche, September 2025) calls the 40B Consilience run the largest distributed pre-training run at that date, without measured results on that page. **Distributed Low-Communication Training with Decoupled Momentum Optimization** (Sasho Nedelkoski et al., 2025, DynaFront workshop at NeurIPS 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03371) reports 16x less communication than DiLoCo.
- **Other compressors.** **PULSE** (Erfan Miahi and Eugene Belilovsky, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03839): over 17x less trainer communication than DiLoCo, in reinforcement learning post-training. **Sign compression for Muon** (Maria Smirnova and Alexey Kravatskiy, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29674): the heuristic placement outperforms the provable one in their experiments. Core Automation uses stochastic rounding with error feedback. Decoupled DiLoCo finds 4-bit about equal to 16-bit, with 2-bit and 1-bit degrading badly. *Complementary.*
- **MT-DAO** (Alex Iacob et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05361) and **LoRDO** (Andrej Jovanović et al., 2026, ICML 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04396). *Complementary.* Local-update versions of adaptive and low-rank optimizers.

### 2.6 Runs and systems

- **The Hitchhiker's Guide to Training on Compute Islands.** Rohan Anil, Core Automation blog, 2 November 2026. https://www.coreauto.com/hitchhikers-guide-to-training-on-compute-islands. *Closest recent work; practitioner source, not peer reviewed.* 160 B300 in the US and 160 GB300 in Asia, 32B MoE (256 experts, top-8), sync every 50 steps by fragments, late updates can commit, compressed exchange over bidirectional 25 Gbps. A second experiment replaces all-reduce with butterfly mixing among eight cells at one site. Two things matter for us. Microbatch and gradient accumulation were tuned so that both sites take similar time per step, so the run does not test unequal step counts. And the post states no aggregation weights, no seeds and no second inner optimizer.
- **OpenDiLoCo** (Sami Jaghouar et al., 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07852): open replication, two continents, 90 to 95% compute utilization. **INTELLECT-1 Technical Report** (Sami Jaghouar et al., 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01152): 10B model, 1 trillion tokens, up to 14 nodes on 3 continents, 400x less bandwidth. **PCCL** (Michael Keiblinger et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14065): up to 45 Gbit/s across Europe and 25 Gbit/s across North America and Europe over TCP. *Tools and precedents.*
- **INTELLECT-2** (2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07291) and **INTELLECT-3** (2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16144) are reinforcement learning runs, not low-communication pretraining. They should not be cited as DiLoCo evidence.
- **Covenant-72B** (Joel Lidin et al., 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08163): 72B with SparseLoCo, about 1.1T tokens, permissionless peers. **Gauntlet** (Joel Lidin et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21684): rewards pseudo-gradient contributions by estimated loss change, a live cousin of quality-based weighting. **DiLoCoX** (Ji Qi et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21263): 107B over 1 Gbps. **Agora** (Gil Avraham et al., 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13332) and **Heterogeneous Low-Bandwidth Pre-Training** (Yazan Obeidi et al., 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02360): pipeline parallel across weak devices, a different family. **torchft** v0.3.1 (github.com/pytorch/torchft). *Complementary.*

## 3. The gaps

- **No factorial.** Replica-scaling papers vary the inner optimizer on identical workers. Heterogeneity papers vary the rule under one inner optimizer. GeoMesh varies the inner optimizer under one rule, without Muon or whitening.
- **No test of the whitening mechanism on mixed workers.** [1] argues the link between whitening and uniform averaging, and measures replica scaling, but with equal workers.
- **Practice has outrun comparison.** Token weights (cross-facility paper), tokens times tokens per step (Decoupled DiLoCo), speed-proportional steps (DyLU, GeoMesh) and speed-proportional batches (Core Automation, cross-facility paper) are all in use, and we found no study that compares them side by side.
- **No seeds on the headline runs.** Core Automation reports none, GeoMesh reports one run per configuration, and the cross-facility paper presents single runs. A careful small study with intervals adds something.
- **No published price to a fixed loss.** We found dollars in none of the papers above. No measured link map between Indian GPU clouds turned up in one targeted search, which is weak evidence of absence.

## Added 4 November, evening: prior work for the language-skew test

Exp02 sent us to shards that differ by language, so we searched for that setting again: eight date-sorted arXiv API queries (DiLoCo with non-IID or multilingual terms, pseudo-gradient weighting, Muon with non-IID terms, federated pretraining) and the abstracts they returned. We read one paper's text closely.

**HeLoCo** (arXiv 2606.00271) is the nearest. In its non-IID setup each of five languages (de, en, es, fr, it) sits on one worker of fixed speed, so language and speed are tied together; English is on the fastest worker, which gives 58.2% of the updates. It is asynchronous, it corrects stale updates, and its conclusions rest on the average loss across languages, with per-language results in figures. It does not name or vary the inner optimizer in the text we read, and it has no worst-language metric. A later test, held for now, would tie language to speed as HeLoCo does, in both directions, on a synchronous schedule and across rules and optimizers. Ablation A keeps them apart.

**Federated learning** has worked on client drift and non-IID weighting for years, so "non-IID aggregation" alone is not new, and a reviewer will know this. **FedMuon** (arXiv 2510.27403) and **NBCLT** (arXiv 2610.17913) bring Muon-like geometry to federated local training; we have read their abstracts only, and they are to be read before the main study, together with FedMIX-P above. What we did not find, as of this search, is a comparison of aggregation rules crossed with Muon or a whitening optimizer on workers that differ in both speed and language, with per-language loss as a primary measure. This is one evening's search, and the check should be repeated before anything is claimed.

## 5. Reading order

**Read first, in this order:**

1. **DiLoCo** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08105). The two-loop method, the vocabulary of inner steps, outer steps and pseudo-gradients, and the 500-times result that everything else builds on. About 1.5 hours.
2. **Asynchronous Local-SGD Training for Language Modeling** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09135). The first study of mixed-speed workers on language models: speed-based local steps, and why outer momentum breaks when updates are stale. About 2 hours.
3. **MuLoCo** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23725). The evidence that the inner optimizer shapes the pseudo-gradient, which is the half of the question that the first two papers leave open. Read sections 1 to 4 and skip the appendices. About 2 hours.

**Then, in week 2:**

4. **How Inner Optimizers Shape Replica Scaling** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19064). The mechanism we are testing. Read section 4.2, Table 3 and the discussion closely. About 3 hours; the theory is the stretch.
5. **The Hitchhiker's Guide to Training on Compute Islands** (https://www.coreauto.com/hitchhikers-guide-to-training-on-compute-islands). The history tables are a good map of the field. About 45 minutes.
6. **GeoMesh** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18388). The closest method and its limits. About 1.5 hours.
7. **Decoupled DiLoCo** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21428), sections on merging and the heterogeneous hardware table. About 1.5 hours.

Optional: FedNova for the bias from unequal steps, and SNOO for the warning that outer momentum alone explains a great deal.

## 7. Open scoping questions for the first discussion
