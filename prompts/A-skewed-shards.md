Ablation A: shards that really differ, split by language.

Read README.md, islands.py, docs/exp02-results.md and results/exp02/decision_rule.json first. Run `python prepare_data.py wiki-en-hi` and compare its hashes with data/wiki_en_hi_manifest.json. Write ablations/a_skew.py. Import from islands.py and do not modify it.

The question: Exp02 found that on i.i.d. shards the inverse-noise estimator carries no information about a worker beyond H_i. When two workers on the same GPU type hold different languages, does the estimator see that, and do inverse-noise weights (R3b) then change the result against token weights (R2)? Do they help the language-balanced loss, and what do they do to each language?

Data. The pool is the same in every condition: 75M English and 75M Hindi training tokens, cut into eight worker shards of 18,743,296 tokens (4,576 blocks of 4,096 tokens, an even number, so that p = 0 is exactly half and half). Workers 0, 2, 4, 6 are English-home and workers 1, 3, 5, 7 are Hindi-home, so each GPU type holds one worker of each language and each language gets exactly half of the tokens. A skew p sets the share of a worker's shard drawn from its home language to 0.5 + 0.5p, so p = 0 is half and half, and p = 1 is home language only. Build each shard from blocks of 4,096 tokens, shuffled with a fixed seed. Pass the result to islands.Data as one array of eight equal shards. About 6% of windows will cross a block boundary; note this in your write-up.

Validation. Use islands.loss_on on en.val and hi.val separately and pass a val_fn to run_islands that returns val_loss (the mean of the two, which we call the balanced loss), en and hi. Validate every 2.5M tokens.

Before any full run, write ablations/A/decision_rule.json, save its SHA-256 to ablations/A/decision_rule.sha256, and have the analysis refuse to run if the file has changed. Include in it, in my words, what I expect to happen and why. Use the tests of results/exp02/decision_rule.json with delta = 0.02 nats: HELPS, HURTS or NO_EFFECT_DETECTED for R3b against R2 on the balanced loss at the budget, in each of p and each optimizer, and the interaction I between AdamW and Muon at each p. Add one manipulation check, the H_ONLY readout of Exp02: for every same-GPU pair, the larger mean s_i^2 over the smaller, over the second half of rounds. Call this C. Under Exp02's i.i.d. shards C was at most 1.015. If C stays at or below 1.10 for every pair in every seed at p = 1, the skew did not reach the estimator, and the run says little about weights under skew. State this outcome in the rule, and report C at p = 0 beside it, since the pool and tokenizer are new. List anything else you read afterward as post hoc, in its own section.

Runs. p in {0, 0.5, 1}, rules R2 and R3b, optimizers AdamW and Muon, seeds 1, 2, 3, 60M tokens per run, inner learning rates from results/exp01/choices/inner.json, the speeds and schedules of islands.py. First tune the outer setting at p = 1 per rule and optimizer over islands.OUTERS, seed 100, 15M tokens, by the balanced loss, and use the same choice at every p. Paired seeds must share the initial weights and every worker's random stream. In all, 36 main runs and 12 tuning runs, about 2.3 billion tokens. On one L40S that is about 5 hours, or $11 to $12 at Modal list prices.

Controls. At equal speeds (H = 10 for all, a short run), R2's final weights on the skewed shards must equal R1's bit for bit, and R3b's must differ. Inject the view bug once and confirm the zero-Delta assert fires. Report the per-worker norms and the mean pairwise cosine of the Delta_i at each p.

Read this before you interpret anything. s_i^2 is the distance between a worker's per-step rate and the aggregate rate, so under skew it mixes noise with disagreement. A language far from the aggregate can lose weight whether its updates are noisy or merely different. So a gain on the balanced loss needs the per-language losses beside it. The inner products in the log (field G) let you split the two after the run: disagreement between language groups against disagreement within a group. Such a split is post hoc, and it should be labelled so.
